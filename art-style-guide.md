# Visual Style Guide: Classic TSR & Vintage Fantasy Oil Art

Use this guide as the baseline reference whenever generating artwork (portraits, scene moments, tokens, or landscape spreads) for our campaign.

---

## 🎨 Aesthetic Intent
We avoid the glossy, hyper-clean digital rendering look typical of modern video games and AI defaults. Our target aesthetic is the traditional, hand-painted fantasy art of the late 1970s through early 1990s TSR-era Dungeons & Dragons (in the tradition of Larry Elmore, Keith Parkinson, and Jeff Easley).

---

## 🖌️ Core Visual Pillars

### 1. Traditional Medium & Texture
* **Medium:** Oil painting on textured canvas or gouache/watercolor wash on heavy illustration board.
* **Surface Quality:** Visible layered brushwork, dry-brush scumbling on stone/fabric, and subtle canvas or print grain.
* **Imperfections:** Avoid glass-smooth gradients. Colors should show textural blending and physical paint buildup.

### 2. Natural Matte Lighting & Palette
* **Color Values:** Earthy undertones, warm ambers, rich shadows, muted secondary tones, and matte surfaces.
* **Lighting Model:** Naturalistic ambient lighting (torchlight, daylight, lantern fire, or storm sky) rather than high-contrast neon rim light or digital bloom.
* **Speculars:** Dull reflections on leather and metal rather than mirror-like chrome or glossy plastic finishes.

### 3. Anatomical Realism & Practical Detailing
* Characters should feel physically grounded in their world. Leather looks lived-in and creased, weapons carry scuffs, and fabrics drape naturally.
* Magical effects (sparks, runes, auras) should look painted with fine brush strokes rather than added as a glowing post-process digital filter.
